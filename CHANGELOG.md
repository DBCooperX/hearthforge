# Changelog

## Unreleased

### Playtest fixes
- **Renaming a table is easy to find.** The table name in the lobby now has a pencil and can be clicked to rename it (hosts and the Innkeeper). The Rename link is easier to read on the parchment.
- **Solo and multiplayer tables are listed apart.** The lobby groups tables into Multiplayer and Solo, each with the most recently played first.
- **Tidy names.** Place names on the zone map no longer overlap: long names are cut short, each name takes a free spot around its place, and one that still finds no room shows when you point at the place. Long hero names are cut with a dot-dot-dot in the party panel and on the battle nameplate, which shows the first name.
- **Table talk chime.** A soft two-note chime plays when another player writes in table talk (never for your own messages). It has its own switch and volume in the sound menu, under Each sound.
- **Table talk is a window.** Drag it by its title bar; pull the grip in its bottom right corner to make it bigger or smaller. Position and size are remembered (double-click the title bar to put it back), and it always stays on screen.
- **Two-weapon fighting.** A light melee weapon (dagger, shortsword, scimitar, handaxe) can go in the off hand next to another light weapon. After attacking, the new Off-hand attack button spends the bonus action on a second strike without the ability modifier.
- **Splitting stacks.** Click a stack, then Split… to take some out into a new stack or hand them to another hero; hold Shift while dropping a stack on a hero tab to hand over part of it.
- **Ready-made heroes in German.** Appearance and backstory of the ready-made heroes show in the table's language.
- Battle map buttons (view and zoom) no longer pass clicks on to the map underneath.

### Story illustrations
- **Hand-drawn pictures of the big moments.** When a boss steps out, a hard fight is won or a big secret comes out, the Dungeon Master can have the moment painted in ink and watercolour; the picture appears above that scene for the whole table and opens large with a click. The default style is a loose black charcoal sketch on old paper. Off by default: the Innkeeper switches it on under Art → Illustrations and picks the service (Google Gemini by default, FLUX on fal.ai or Replicate, OpenAI), the model, the style, which moments, and limits per table, per day and per month. Keys go under Art → Switches and keys and work without a restart. Pictures go on the player credits as their own column. Heroes and people are drawn from the looks the game keeps, and earlier pictures of the table go along so they stay recognisable. See README → Story illustrations.
- **Pictures are drawn stroke by stroke.** A scene picture is no longer faded in: it is drawn on aged paper in about 6 seconds. Strokes first trace the picture's real edges (found in your browser), then the dark marks fill in, then the shading comes in band by band from the darkest to the lightest tones, in cross-hatching, with a soft pencil tap at the start and pencil scritches while it draws, on the sound-effects channel (mute it there). A click while it draws shows it at once. With reduced motion in your system settings it simply appears. It all happens in your browser, so it costs nothing extra.
- **A picture for a new place.** The first time the party arrives at a place it has never been (a village, a ruin, a tavern), the Dungeon Master can have it drawn. It is on by default as the moment "The party arrives at a new place for the first time" (Innkeeper → Art → Illustrations); the server only allows it on that first arrival, never on a revisit, and the usual limits per table and day and the gap between pictures still apply.
- **Pictures and voice in the AI costs.** Innkeeper → AI costs (the totals on the overview, per table, per day and per model) now count what story pictures and the paid narrator voices cost, next to the Dungeon Master's tokens: new Pictures and Voice columns, two new tiles, and rows for them under By model. They were only on the players' credits before. Costs from before this update are not added in afterwards.
- **Painted hero portraits.** With illustrations on, every new hero gets a painted portrait in colour from the choices in the character creator, all in one shared look made for the round brass frames. It replaces the drawn bust once it is ready (the drawn one stays as the fallback), a changed look gets a new one, and the player whose hero it is pays for it (at most 3 a day each by default).
- **Painted battle figures.** With illustrations on, the figures on the battle map are painted in the same look as the portraits, in both the 2.5D and the flat view. Each hero's figure is painted from their portrait so they match (paid by its player); each kind of monster is painted once for the whole server and reused, so a goblin costs one picture however many goblins come (paid by the table where it first appears). The drawn figures stay as the fallback until a painted one is ready. Switch, a daily limit (20 by default) and a sample under Innkeeper → Art → Illustrations → Battle figures; the cost shows in AI costs and on the player credits like other pictures.

### Combat
- **End turn reminder.** On your turn in a fight, after 20 seconds with nothing happening, the End turn button starts to glow and a reminder appears; a few seconds after your action and movement are used up it comes sooner. The turn never ends by itself. Set the delay (off, 20, 45 or 90 seconds) in Settings → Game.
- **"Your turn" in the middle of the map.** When one of your heroes comes up in a fight, a large "Your turn" banner (German "Du bist dran") appears in the middle of the battle map for a moment, with a short bell; if you play several heroes it names the one whose turn it is. The end turn reminder ("Still there?") appears there too, large and in the middle.
- **Whose turn it is, over the figure.** A bobbing tag with an arrow floats above whoever is acting on the battle map, in 2.5D and flat: a bright brass "Your turn" ("Du bist dran") over your own hero, brass "Now acting" ("Am Zug") over other heroes, red over monsters. It sits above any status icons.

### Fixes
- **The clock matches the opening.** An adventure no longer always starts at 08:00 on day 1: a festival opens at dusk (19:00), a shipwreck at dawn (06:00), your own opening and a campaign book's start at the time of day they name ("one evening", "im Morgengrauen"), and books read from now on note the hour their first scene plays at. The Dungeon Master is told when the adventure began and, at book tables, to move the clock with the story instead of saying it is evening while the map shows morning.

### Maintenance
- Cleanup before the mobile work: unused code, 90 unused German strings and about 460 lines of stale or overridden CSS removed (theme.css folded into styles.css, screens pixel-identical), duplicated helpers shared, and the type check now fails on unused imports and variables.

### Story
- **Your hero's backstory matters.** The Dungeon Master now gets clear rules for the backstory you write in the creator: it treats it as true, gives every hero one personal thread from their past tied to a person, place or quest of the story, and pays it off at key moments without letting one hero take over. Before the first scene (and when a hero joins later) a short call on the cheapest model drafts each hero's thread from the region, or from the campaign book's own people and places, for the Dungeon Master only. Backstories now sit in the cached part of the prompt, so they no longer cost full price every turn.
- **Play a published adventure.** Under Where, pick a campaign book instead of a rolled region: drop in a PDF, text or Markdown file of an adventure you own, or take one from a short list of free adventures (Peril in Pinebrook downloads with one click straight from Wizards of the Coast; for A Most Potent Brew, The Wild Sheep Chase, Intro to Stormwreck Isle, Frozen Sick, the One Page Dungeon Contest and the Basic Fantasy modules, "Get it here" opens the source and you drop the file). The server reads the book once into sections and a digest, starts the map with the book's town, places, people and first quest, and the Dungeon Master follows the book's plot while reading only the section the party is in. See README → Campaign books.

### Interface
- **Master volume.** The sound menu (speaker button, and Settings → Audio) now opens with one master slider that turns every sound up or down at once: music, ambient sound, effects, the narrator and the interface. The sliders for each sound fold away under "Each sound", with small icons that show which ones are off; each is a share of the master volume. Your choice is remembered in the browser.
- **The game cursor now shows in the game too**, not only on the landing page. The story and the chat had switched back to the browser pointer.
- **Polish:** the Innkeeper's Save bar no longer lets headings show through; story tone, challenge, content rating, thinking depth and map tiles show their names (in German too) instead of raw values such as "heroic" or "3"; the Players section has room between accounts and "Who is connected"; Settings shows voice input as "Available" instead of an "On" that looked like a switch; German species traits and spell effects give distances in metres.
- **The Innkeeper can read a table's whole story.** Each table in the Innkeeper's Tables list has a Story button: quests with their notes, the story's open questions with the clues found, held-back secrets, people met, places found (with dungeon progress), the timeline, factions, rumours, the journal and the running summary. What the party does not know yet (the Dungeon Master's premise, held-back secrets, undiscovered places and people, planned events) stays hidden until "Show what the party does not know yet" is ticked, so the Innkeeper can read along without spoilers. English and German.
- **Esc opens a game menu**, as in any PC role-playing game: Resume, The adventure (story style, challenge, content, story language), Party and invites, Settings, Your account, Innkeeper, Art credits, Fullscreen, Leave the table and, for the host, New adventure. The same menu (with a menu button top right) works on the list of tables and in the table room. Windows opened from it lead back to it on Esc. The gear button and the language switch in the top bar are gone; the language is under Settings → Game.
- **Settings has three tabs:** Game, Audio and Keyboard. The table, account and server entries moved into the menu.
- **The game's own confirm dialogs** replace the browser's pop-up boxes everywhere (removing a player, deleting a table, restarting the server, a new adventure…). Enter confirms, Esc cancels.
- **Fullscreen** from the menu. In Chrome and Edge Esc still opens the menu while in fullscreen (hold Esc to leave it).
- **New tables in two simple steps.** The lobby asks only how many players and opens the table. In the table room, "Your adventure" shows five cards, always visible: Where, How it begins, Story style, Challenge and Content, each with its choice and a line saying what it means. A click opens a picker with the full descriptions. The table's name, story language, invite-only and world seed sit under "Table details". The old chip bar, "Customize", its two tabs and the lobby's "More options" are gone. Players who do not host see the same cards, read-only.
- **Challenge explained on screen.** The rules slider is now a five-step "Challenge" picker with a table under it that shows, for every level, what changes: check difficulty, warnings, failed checks, rules hints, how enemies fight and how likely a hero's death is. The chosen level is lit.
- **Challenge changes how monsters fight.** On Coaching and Gentle monsters spread their attacks over the healthiest heroes; on Firm and Strict they gang up on the most wounded one they can reach. The Dungeon Master is told so before every monster turn, and the server follows it when the Dungeon Master names no target. Heroes at 0 hit points are never attacked, as before.
- In the game, the menu's "The adventure" shows the same cards (story style, challenge, content, story language).
- **The Innkeeper is sorted by feature.** Eight sections instead of nine, and no more general "Settings" tab: every switch and key now sits next to what it controls. Costs and credits; Dungeon Master (Anthropic key and its test, models, thinking depth, what new tables start with, the timers at the table); Sound and voice (music, ambient sounds, effects and narrator libraries, plus their on/off switches and the Freesound, ElevenLabs and OpenAI keys); Art (art packs and their switches); Server (updates, HTTPS, restart and password, the server settings, backups and the log).
- **Table talk no longer jumps.** The chat keeps a fixed height and only its own lines scroll, never the table room around it. If you scrolled up to read, it stays there and offers "New messages". Lines from the same person are grouped, with the time.
- **From battle back to the story, step by step.** The victory or defeat card stays until you press Continue (no timer) and says whether the Dungeon Master is still writing ("The dust settles…") or the story is ready. Then the card lifts, the battle map sinks and blurs away, the story slides back into the middle, the music fades over four seconds instead of cutting, and an "After the battle" line marks where the aftermath begins.
- **A game look instead of a web page.** What you type now goes on a parchment command bar with a quill; the suggested actions carry their number, and the keys 1 to 9 pick them. Own mouse pointer, gold focus rings, iron scrollbars, game-styled drop-downs, check boxes and sliders. Buttons and labels can no longer be selected by accident (story, chat and fields still can), and screens fade in instead of snapping.

### Combat
- **Smarter monster movement.** Archers and casters now pick where to stand: a square they can shoot their target from, out of arm's reach, behind a tree, rock or crate when there is one, instead of standing in the open or shooting point-blank with Disadvantage. Goblins slip out of reach with Nimble Escape before shooting. "Hold position" may still take a step or two into cover. Creatures backing off look for distance and cover. Melee monsters walk through their allies' squares (but never stop on them), so a creature stuck behind a friend in a doorway or corridor no longer just stands there; if its target is walled in, it goes for a hero it can reach, or at least closes in. Fliers such as bats, stirges and dragons move at their flying speed.
- **Attack and spell animations always play.** The blow that ends a fight (a Fire Bolt on the last goblin) now plays on the battle map before the victory or defeat card rises, instead of the map being swapped out under it. A creature felled mid-fight stays until the hit reaches it, with its damage number, then fades. Saves against breath weapons, poisons, charms and holds show a ring too, not only spell saves that deal damage.

### Accounts
- **Rename accounts.** Players rename themselves in their profile; the Innkeeper renames any account, the default "innkeeper" too, from Players → Accounts. Seats at tables follow the new name (unless someone there already has it), and sessions stay signed in. The password-recovery file now finds the owner even after a rename.
- **Clear your stats.** In the profile: reset all numbers and the hero list, clear the whole list, or remove one hero's entry (the hero stays at the table). The Innkeeper can clear any account's stats.
- **German says "Innkeeper"**, not "Wirt", for the owner's panel and role; the group panel heading is "Party".

### Costs
- **Lower AI bills through prompt caching.** The Dungeon Master's prompt is now laid out so Claude can reuse most of it: the system prompt and tools stay cached for an hour, then come the table's fixed background, the recent story (which now only grows at the end, one entry at a time, and moves on in steps), and only then the live state and what the players did. Each follow-up call inside a turn (after every tool the DM uses) reads the whole turn from the cache instead of paying for it again. One-off background calls are no longer marked for caching. The cost report prices hour-long cache writes at twice the input price.
- **Player credits.** Under Innkeeper → AI costs, book what each player has paid in, in francs. Every call to Claude and every sentence a paid narrator voice reads (ElevenLabs or OpenAI; Piper is free) is shared among the accounts sitting at the table and taken off their credit at your rate of francs per dollar. Players see their credit in their profile, get an early warning when it runs low, and once it is used up they cannot ask the Dungeon Master for anything new (a fight under way is finished) until you top it up. Credits are off by default; costs are counted per account either way. Innkeepers, and accounts you mark free, are never stopped.

### Voice
- **Speak instead of typing.** A microphone button beside the story's text box: hold it (or `Ctrl+M`) and talk, let go, and the words land in the box to fix before sending. A tap starts and a second tap stops. The browser's own speech recognition, in the player's interface language (German tuned for Swiss speakers, written with "ss"). Chrome, Edge and Safari; hidden in Firefox and on plain http, and Settings says why.
- **A narrator reads the story aloud.** Every new Dungeon Master scene is read as it arrives, while the music steps back; a small speaker on each scene card reads it again or stops. Each player has a mute button and volume slider next to the music. The Innkeeper picks the voice for the whole server under Assets → Narrator, with Listen buttons: **Piper** runs free on the server (in the image; its voices are downloaded from Piper's collection the first time), or **OpenAI** and **ElevenLabs** with an API key under Settings → Voice. Speech is cached on the server, long scenes are read in parts so they start quickly, and only the table's own story can be spoken.
- **HTTPS with Caddy.** `COMPOSE_PROFILES=https` starts Caddy in front of the game: Let's Encrypt for a public domain, a DNS challenge for domains only your network knows, or Caddy's own local certificate for the server's IP, which each device trusts once from `/hearthforge-ca.crt`. Set it up in the Innkeeper under Server & updates → HTTPS (the updater writes `.env` and starts Caddy), or let the Proxmox installer do it (local certificate by default).

### Look
- **2.5D battles, closer up.** The battle map is now a tilted, lit table seen at an angle with perspective: heroes and monsters stand on it as painted miniatures on round bases (brass rims for heroes, red for monsters) with soft shadows, a slow breath, and health bars over their heads. Arrows fly, spells streak in coloured by their damage type, blades leave a slash, failed saves bloom in a ring, healing rises in green motes, and the slain fade into the floor. Damage numbers wait until the blow lands, just after the dice in the story settle. The grid is zoomed in by default and the camera follows whoever is acting, leaning towards the foe they face. A toolbar in the corner of the map switches between **2.5D** and the classic **flat** view and sets the zoom (also with the mouse wheel); both are remembered per browser. The board lies on a candlelit tavern table, in the middle of a worn rug among plates, tankards, coins, dice and character sheets, tilted with the same perspective; zoom out (down to 60%) to see the whole table. In a dark fight the table dims too. **Look around** when zoomed in: point at the edge of the battle map (after a short pause, so crossing it does nothing), drag the map, or hold Shift with the arrow keys or WASD; the new ⌖ button brings the view back, and so does the next turn. Edge scrolling can be switched off in the settings. With "reduce motion" the effects become simple flashes and the camera stops gliding, and slower computers automatically get a lighter version of the 2.5D view.
- **Heroes and monsters drawn in ink.** The generated characters, monsters and portraits no longer look like a child's pixel drawing. They are painted at high resolution like ink illustrations in an old rulebook: shaded forms with ink contours, muted earthy colours, cross-hatching in the shadows and a heavy outer line. Heroes have heroic proportions and real faces (brows, eyes, nose, mouth), dragonborn get a proper dragon's head, and monsters get scowling brows, jaws and bending legs. Every token and portrait sits in a round frame: brass with rivets for heroes, blackened iron for monsters. The party panel, hero tabs, lobby, character sheet, creator and hover cards show head-and-shoulders portraits; the inventory keeps the whole figure. On the battle map the creature whose turn it is gets a brass nameplate on its token. Everything is still drawn by the game itself, so nothing is downloaded and every look the creator or the Dungeon Master picks still shows.
- **Livelier heroes and monsters.** The ink figures now have curved, tapering limbs and rounded bodies instead of straight segments, and light falls on them from the upper left so arms, torsos and heads look round. Each surface is drawn as what it is: polished steel with a highlight, chainmail rings, fur, dragon scales, wood grain, cloth folds and leather. Heroes stand like their class: casters raise a staff with a spell glowing in the other hand, rogues, rangers and monks crouch with the blade forward, fighters, paladins and clerics brace behind the shield, and barbarians fight bare-chested under a fur mantle. Faces are turned three-quarter, and each hero's name settles small details like the jaw, nose, brows, a scar or freckles, so two humans no longer look alike. Monsters are ready to strike: weapons raised overhead, beasts with a paw lifted and the jaw open, dragons with open jaws and real horns. Several monsters of one kind in a fight now differ a little in build, shade, clothing and scars.
- **Art polish: closer to a hand-drawn look.**
  - Ink lines are thin on the lit side and heavier in shadow, and outlines tremble slightly, like pen work.
  - Portraits are now painted at full size instead of being cut out of a small figure, so faces are crisp.
  - Faces are clean of hatching. They have lower lids and expressions to match the class: fighters frown, rogues smirk, casters stay calm and barbarians grin with bared teeth.
  - Hair has a side parting, a lock swept across the brow and visible strands, and wild hair is tousled instead of spiky.
  - Hands close round their weapon grips, and spellcasters hold an open palm under their spell.
  - Bears, owlbears and giant spiders get bodies of their own: a humped bear with a pale muzzle and roaring jaws, an owlbear with a feathered ruff, ear tufts, staring eyes and a hooked beak, and a spider with eight jointed legs, a marked abdomen, eight eyes and fangs.
  - Skeletons are real bone (ribcage, spine, pelvis, knobbed joints and claws).
  - Zombies and ghouls reach out with clawed hands, in rags torn open over their ribs.
  - The Dungeon Master can use `body:bear`, `body:owlbear` and `body:spider` when restyling a creature.
- **A tavern behind the table.** The lobby, the table room and the character creator now sit in front of a candlelit tavern table: darkened, softly blurred and vignetted so the forms stay readable, with the panels turned into frosted iron that lets the firelight through. A very slow drift and candle flicker bring it to life (off with "reduce motion"); the creator pushes it further back. Phones get a tall crop of the party. The picture is WebP with a JPEG fallback.

### Playing
- **Test mode.** In the Innkeeper's Tables page the owner can switch a table into test mode. The table is saved as it stands, a "Test mode" badge shows in the top bar for everyone, and the host gets a toolbox (click the badge). **Fight:** pick creatures from the whole catalogue or let the server draw a group for a challenge budget, and start normally, with the heroes surprising the enemies (optionally with one hero striking first from hiding) or with the enemies ambushing the party. **Heroes:** level up, give experience, set hit points (down to knocked out), revive, put on or lift conditions, refill mana and class resources, hand out items and gold. **World:** jump to a known place and a spot on its zone map, reveal the whole region, move the clock (+1 hour, +4 hours, +1 day, or to midnight, dawn, noon, dusk), take a short or long rest that always works. **Scene:** tell the Dungeon Master what should happen next ("a merchant appears"); the hint never shows in the story. Ending test mode in the Innkeeper either keeps what the tests changed or puts the story back to how it was when test mode began. There is no weather in the game yet, so none can be set.
- **Zone maps.** Inside a village, a marsh or a dungeon, the side panel now shows an inked map of the place itself, with the region's hex map one tab away (also in the big map window, M). It marks the spots the story has named (the toll house, the jetty, the reeds), the paths between them, where the party stands and what is still in the fog. The Dungeon Master names spots as the scene moves, and the background check after each reply follows the party when the narration walks it somewhere. Taverns and other places inside a town are on it from the start, a click walks you in, and a dungeon shows its sections as far as you have got.
- **Ask the Dungeon Master out of character.** Start a line with "to DM:", "an SL:", "Spielleiter:" or "OOC:" (or press "Ask the DM" beside the input) and the answer comes back as a plain grey note, not a story card. It does not move the story on, uses no turn and does not hold up the other players. If your question reveals a mistake, such as a missing coin, the Dungeon Master puts the game right and says so. At a shared table everyone sees the question and the note.
- **A story you can follow.** Every scene card names where it plays, the time of day and who is there. The Dungeon Master stages each scene and tags every speaker, explains what the heroes would plainly know, adds at most one new mystery per scene, and lets people answer what they are asked instead of pushing hooks. Clues the party finds are filed in a new Clues tab of the journal, grouped by open question.
- **A cleaner combat hotbar.** Square slots framed by what they spend (green action, orange bonus action, purple reaction), with the turn's action, bonus action, reaction, movement and mana shown above. Drag spells and features from the spellbook or the character sheet onto the bar, drag them off or right-click to clear a slot; the layout is kept per hero. Every greyed-out button says why on hover, and reaction spells such as Shield are marked as casting themselves.
- **Surprise and strikes from hiding.** Sneak up on enemies with a successful Stealth check and the fight opens with them caught unawares: each enemy whose Passive Perception your Stealth beats rolls initiative with Disadvantage (SRD 5.2.1). A hero who crept up to strike first is set down behind the target, takes the first turn, and the attack is rolled at once with Advantage and Sneak Attack. Enemies can ambush the party the same way. The server checks the dice, so the Dungeon Master cannot hand out surprise without a roll. A banner announces it, and the initiative bar marks who was caught unawares.
- **A victory screen.** When the last enemy falls, the battle map stays a moment under a "Victory!" banner with experience, gold, what was found (also what the Dungeon Master hands out right after) and level-ups, then fades back to the story. Being overwhelmed or falling gets a matching screen.
- **Status icons over the tokens.** Hunter's Mark, Hex, Bless, concentration and conditions show above each token on the battle map, with turns left and how they end on hover.
- **Story and game stay in step.** When the story walks the party back to town, lets it rest, or has a hero pocket, leave behind or draw something, the game follows: map, music, clock, inventory and gear. The Dungeon Master has tools for story travel, rests and gear, and a quick background check after each reply catches what it narrated without them. Resting an hour or more is a short rest, a night's sleep a long rest, and a note says what came back. "Who is here" lists the people the scene header names.
- **Sound effects.** Sword, arrow and spell hits (by damage type), misses, footsteps, taking damage, healing, falling, monsters dying, the start of a fight, victory, defeat, level-ups, coins and quiet button clicks. A new volume slider with its own mute icon sits next to the music and ambient ones; the Innkeeper can switch all effects off (`HEARTHFORGE_SFX`). Every effect is made by the browser, so nothing has to be installed; for more natural hits, footsteps and coins the owner can add Kenney's free CC0 packs under Assets → Sound effects (open Kenney's page, download the zip, drop it in). Another part of the app can request any sound with `window.dispatchEvent(new CustomEvent('hearthforge:sfx', { detail: 'victory' }))`.

- **Believable timing.** The Dungeon Master keeps a timeline on the world clock: when something from the past first comes up ("three weeks ago the mayor sent two guards to the mill"), it gets a fixed date and keeps it, along with who knows about it. The DM is told to avoid timing coincidences without a cause, to let people know only what they saw or were told, and to hold big revelations back until their condition is met; a revelation can also carry an earliest time, and the server refuses it before then. Planned events (a barge due in two days) show as due once the clock reaches them. The background check after each reply dates anything the DM told without the tool.

### Server
- **Art, music and effects are the owner's call, and everyone follows live.** Item icons, map tiles, music, ambient loops and sound effects are chosen only in the Innkeeper and apply to every table on the server; players pick nothing and keep only their own volumes and mutes. When the owner changes something, every open browser is told: icons, music and effects swap in at once, and a new map tile pack reloads the page by itself when nobody is typing or seated (otherwise a "Reload" button appears). Nothing had to be migrated: no art choice was ever kept per browser.
- **Kenney art packs are added by the owner.** Kenney's site refuses automatic downloads, so the Tiny Dungeon tile pack now works like the sound packs: Innkeeper → Assets shows "Download from Kenney" (opens the pack's page) and you drop the zip onto the window. The misleading "Downloads straight from kenney.nl" line and the failing start-up download are gone; without the zip the game keeps its built-in pixel art. game-icons.net still downloads by itself.
- **Every story turn on Opus, for a comparison.** A new Innkeeper switch, "Every story turn on the key-moment model" (`HEARTHFORGE_DM_STORY_ON_KEY`), runs all narration and replies outside fights on the key-moment model (Opus 5.5 by default) instead of only turning points. Background work and monster turns stay on their own models.
- **Browsers pick up updates.** The server tells every browser which client build it serves. A tab that is still on the old build reloads itself when nobody sits at a table and nothing is typed, and otherwise shows an "Update ready" button. Seats and names survive the reload. A missing script or style file now answers 404 instead of the page, so an old tab can no longer load the wrong thing.

## 0.9.0 (15 November 2026): first playtest release

The first version meant for real play-testing with the live AI Dungeon Master. Features that have only been tested against the scripted fallback and the unit tests are listed in [docs/PLAYTEST.md](docs/PLAYTEST.md).

### Rules content
- **Monsters.** All 341 monsters of the SRD 5.2.1 with multiattack, breath weapons and area effects, conditions, regeneration, resistances, Magic and Legendary Resistance and legendary actions. The Dungeon Master searches the catalogue by name, type and Challenge (#28).
- **Classes.** All twelve SRD classes, levels 1 to 20, subclasses at level 3, feats, epic boons and the class features with their own counters (#29).
- **Spells and items.** All 339 spells and 239 magic items, attunement, wands and staffs with charges, hands and belt slots (#30).
- **Icons** for skills, feats, spells and class features, from game-icons.net (#32).

### Playing
- **Open world.** Hex overworld, scene cards, DM memory and progression (#2). Battle maps that match the place of the fight (#6).
- **Combat rules.** Opportunity attacks with a per-player reaction prompt, Disengage, light levels and darkvision (#27).
- **Reputation.** Faction standing, rumours, trophies and undo (#26).
- **Merchants.** Shops with real stock, buying, selling and haggling (#23).
- **Start options.** Random regions, premises and a shareable world seed (#16).
